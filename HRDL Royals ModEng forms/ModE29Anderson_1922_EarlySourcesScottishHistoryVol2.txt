When king Alexander lay in Kerrera sound, he dreamed a dream and he thought that three men came to him. He thought that one wore royal apparel; this man was very frowning, and red-faced, and stout in figure. The second man seemed to him tall, and slender, and youthful; the fairest of men, and nobly dressed. The third was by far the largest in figure, and the most frowning, of them all. He was very bald in front. He spoke to the king, and asked whether he intended to go plundering to the Hebrides. He thought he answered that that was certain. The dream-man bade him turn back, and said to him that they would not hear of anything else. The king told his dream; and men begged him to turn back, but he would not do that. And but little later he fell ill, and died. Then the Scots broke up the levy, and conveyed the king's body up into Scotland. The Hebrideans say that these men who appeared to the king in his sleep [must] have been St Olaf, king of Norway; and St Magnus, earl of the Orkneys; and St Columba.
