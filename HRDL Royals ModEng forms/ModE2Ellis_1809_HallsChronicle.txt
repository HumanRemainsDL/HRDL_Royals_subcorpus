When Atropos had cut the line of his [Richard II's] life, his body was embalmed and seared and covered with lead all save his face (to the intent that all men might perceive that he was departed out of this mortal life) and was conueyed to London, where in the cathedral church of saint Paul he had a solemn funeral [lit. obsequies], and from thence conueyed to Lagley in Buckinghamshire, where he was interred, and afterwards by king Henry the V removed to Westminster, and there entombed honourably with queen Anne his wife, although the Scots untruly write that he escaped out of prison, and led a virtuous and solitary life in Scotland, and there died and is buried in the black Friars at Stirling.
