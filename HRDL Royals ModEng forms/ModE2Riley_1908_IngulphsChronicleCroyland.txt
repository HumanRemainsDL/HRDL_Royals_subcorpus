The afore-named king Ethelbald, after a reign of forty-one years, having rashly engaged in battle at Seggeswold, with the tyrant Bemred, was there slain, in accordance with a prophecy of the holy father, Guthlac. The tyrant Bemred, however, had not long to glory in his excessive tyranny, for he perished in the same year. King Ethelbald was buried at Ripadium, or Ripedune, which was at that time a very celebrated monastery, and, with the consent of the nobles of the whole of Mercia, left the kingdom of the Mercians to Offa, grandson to his uncle by the father's side. Offa was the son of Bignfert, the son of Enulph, the son of Osmod, the son of Eoppa, the son of Wibba, father of king Penda.
