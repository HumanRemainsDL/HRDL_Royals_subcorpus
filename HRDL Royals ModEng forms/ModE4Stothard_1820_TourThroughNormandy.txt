Richard [I], provoked at the refusal of a subject, advanced immediately towards Chalus, determined to besiege the castle; and whilst reconnoitering before the walls, received that wound of which he died, A.D. 1199. Before his death he expressed an earnest wish to be interred by the side of his father, Henry; bitterly and deeply lamenting that he had been the guilty cause of that sorrow which had brought the King to an unhappy end, by the cruel rebellion of his children. Richard Coeur de Lion was buried at Fontevraud: his bowels were deposited in the church of Poitiers, and his heart in the cathedral of Rouen. His epitaph was as follows: —

Pictavus exta ducis sepelit, tellusque Chalutis,  
Corpus dat claudi sub marmore Fontis-Ebraldi  
Neustria, tuque tegis cor inexpugnabile Regis;  
Sic loca per trina se sparsit tanta ruina,  
Nec fuit hoc funus cui sufficeret locus unus.

['The Poitevin and earth of Chalus buried the entrails of the leader,
The body closed under the marble of Fontevraud,
Normandy, and you cover the impregnable heart of the king;
Thus so great a fall scattered you through three places,
There was no funeral for which a single place might have sufficed.']
